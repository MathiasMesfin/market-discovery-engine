## 1. Executive Summary & Classification

The core hypothesis of **Project Meridian** is:
> *Around the annual Russell US index reconstitution, stocks exposed to unusually large predictable passive-index forced flows relative to their normal trading liquidity experience abnormal price pressure between the public identification of the rebalance (preliminary list publication) and the effective reconstitution date (fourth Friday in June).*

### Classification: **`DIRECT_PRIOR_ART`**

The specific price pressure generated by the annual Russell 1000/2000 reconstitution breakpoint has been directly, extensively, and rigorously studied in academic financial economics and quantitative finance since the early 2000s.

Specifically:
1. **Chang, Hong, and Liskovich (2015, *Review of Financial Studies*)** directly analyzed the asymmetric index flow around the Russell 1000/2000 cutoff rank using a Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD). They documented that moving from Russell 1000 to Russell 2000 creates massive net forced buying, whereas moving from Russell 2000 to Russell 1000 creates net forced selling.
2. **Petajisto (2011, *Journal of Financial Economics*)** documented the "index premium" and measured the hidden cost to index funds (38–77 bps/year for Russell 2000 funds) caused by market participants front-running predictable reconstitution flows.
3. **Madhavan (2003, *Financial Analysts Journal*)** first established "The Russell Reconstitution Effect", documenting large pre-effective price pressure and subsequent post-effective reversals.
4. **FTSE Russell (2007)** officially acknowledged this widespread arbitrage and introduced the **$\pm 2.5\%$ Market Capitalization Banding Rule** specifically to curb turnover and reduce price distortions around the 1,000 cutoff rank.

Because the exact flow-intensity mechanism, index-weight asymmetry, and announcement-to-effective window have been published in top peer-reviewed journals, **Project Meridian cannot claim absolute novelty.**

└──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────┘

1. **Generic S&P 500 Additions/Deletions:** S&P changes are determined by an active committee and frequently convey fundamental certification or earnings information (Harris & Gurel 1986; Shleifer 1986; Chen, Noronha & Singal 2004).
2. **Russell Rank Discontinuity Studies:** Corporate governance and asset pricing literature uses the rank 1,000 threshold as an instrument for passive institutional ownership (Appel, Gormley & Keim 2016; Coles, Hertzel & Kalpathy 2020).
3. **Russell 1000/2000 Weight Asymmetry:** Because Russell 1000 is cap-weighted among large caps, the 1,000th stock has negligible weight ($\sim 0.005\%$). Because Russell 2000 is cap-weighted among small caps, the 1,001st stock has a massive relative weight ($\sim 0.35\%$). Dropping from R1000 into R2000 is an *inclusion shock* for small-cap passive funds (IWM), requiring substantial net buying.
4. **Flow-to-ADV Intensity (Project Meridian):** Normalizing the expected dollar flow by the stock's 20-day average daily volume ($\text{ADV20}$) to test whether cross-sectional variation in liquidity tightness predicts the magnitude of pre-reconstitution abnormal returns.

## 3. Academic Literature Review

### A. Foundational Studies
1. **Chang, Yen-Cheng, Harrison Hong, and Inessa Liskovich (2015)**
   * *Title:* "Regression Discontinuity and the Price Effects of Stock Market Indexing"
   * *Journal:* *Review of Financial Studies*, Vol. 28, No. 1, pp. 212–246.
   * *Key Finding:* Confirmed that index additions to Russell 2000 experience substantial positive price pressure in June leading into reconstitution day due to index-fund benchmark demand.
2. **Petajisto, Antti (2011)**
   * *Title:* "The Index Premium and Its Hidden Cost for Index Funds"
   * *Journal:* *Journal of Financial Economics*, Vol. 100, No. 2, pp. 271–293.
   * *Key Finding:* Quantified the price distortion caused by predictable index turnover, showing that Russell 2000 tracking funds lose 38–77 bps annually due to front-running.
3. **Madhavan, Ananth (2003)**
   * *Title:* "The Russell Reconstitution Effect"
   * *Journal:* *Financial Analysts Journal*, Vol. 59, No. 4, pp. 51–64.
   * *Key Finding:* Demonstrated that predictable turnover creates temporary excess volume and price impact that partially reverses post-reconstitution.

## 4. Current Market Context & Arbitrage Decay

Following the introduction of the FTSE Russell **$\pm 2.5\%$ banding rule** in 2007 and the maturation of quantitative execution algorithms, much of the historical reconstitution price pressure has been compressed, front-run into May (before the preliminary list), or absorbed by multi-strategy quantitative funds.

Phase 0 tests whether an actionable ex-ante **Flow-to-ADV Intensity** factor survives between the first preliminary list publication in June and the effective reconstitution close across the 2012–2024 sample period.
